Qatar Itinerary: 5-7 Days for First-Time Visitors

Real Doha venues, in an order that actually works, with the kind of price ranges you can budget against instead of guess at.

Venues and price ranges below come from Rihla's own curated Doha database, not scraped listings. Prices are per person unless noted, and swing with season and events: treat them as planning anchors, not quotes. Last checked: September 2026.

Five days is enough to see Doha properly without rushing. Seven gives you room for a slower pace, a second desert trip, or a lazy day by the water. This lays out six core days: treat the last one or two as flexible, and add or drop from there.

Day 1

Old Doha: Souq Waqif & Msheireb

  • Souq Waqif: the historic market, free to wander, best after sunset when the stalls and falcon souq come alive.
  • Msheireb Museums: four restored heritage houses, free entry, a quiet counterpoint to the souq's noise.
  • Dinner: Damasca One for Syrian mezze (roughly QAR 60–150) or Parisa Souq Waqif for the mirrored Persian dining room (roughly QAR 100–250).
Day 2

Museums & the Corniche

  • Museum of Islamic Art: the I.M. Pei-designed landmark on the Corniche; free for residents, roughly QAR 50 for tourists.
  • National Museum of Qatar: the desert-rose building next door; similar tourist pricing, worth the queue.
  • Doha Corniche walk: the 7km bay promenade, free, best at sunset with the West Bay skyline lighting up.
  • Dinner: Jiwan at the National Museum for contemporary Qatari cuisine (roughly QAR 150–400), or splurge at IDAM by Alain Ducasse atop MIA (roughly QAR 350–800).
Day 3

Desert Safari & the Inland Sea

  • Desert safari with dune bashing (Sealine): a half-day 4x4 tour, roughly QAR 250–600 depending on group size and whether it's shared or private; sunset departures are the ones to book.
  • Inland Sea (Khor Al Adaid): the UNESCO-recognized spot where the desert meets the sea, usually bundled into the same tour, roughly QAR 300–700.
  • This is a full day away from the city. Plan a light dinner back in Doha, or ask your tour operator about a desert camp dinner option.
Day 4

Katara & The Pearl

  • Katara Cultural Village: free to explore, with galleries, a small-fee beach, and a strong restaurant scene.
  • The Pearl-Qatar (Porto Arabia): the marina promenade, free to walk, lined with cafés, boutiques and yachts.
  • Lusail Boulevard: Lusail's evening strip if you have energy left, free, often with events running.
  • Dinner: L'wzaar Seafood Market in Katara for pick-your-fish dining (roughly QAR 150–350) or SMAT for traditional Qatari machboos (roughly QAR 80–200).
Day 5

Modern Doha & Family Time

  • Aspire Park & the Torch: a large public park, free, a good slower morning if the last few days have been full.
  • 3-2-1 Qatar Olympic and Sports Museum: interactive and a strong pick if you're traveling with kids, roughly QAR 0–50.
  • Villaggio Mall or Place Vendôme Mall: air-conditioned shopping with a Venice-themed indoor canal or an evening fountain show, both free to enter.
Day 6-7 (if you have them)

A Slower Pace, or One More Day Trip

  • Banana Island day pass: ferry plus resort access for a beach day, roughly QAR 250–500.
  • Al Zubarah Fort: a UNESCO site about 100km north, free entry, needs a car or a day-trip driver.
  • Purple Island (Al Khor): mangrove walks with optional kayaking, a quieter nature morning.

What this actually costs

Add up the ranges above and a 5-day trip built around mid-range choices (not the fine-dining splurges, not the cheapest karak stall either) typically lands somewhere in the QAR 1,200–2,500 per-person range for activities and food, before hotels and flights. That's a wide range on purpose: the whole reason Rihla exists is that a fixed number here would be more confident than honest.
