Visa & entry requirements

GCC nationals travel visa-free; most other nationalities can apply online through Qatar's official Hayya platform or get a visa on arrival. Health insurance is tied to the visa itself, not a separate formality. Start here before you book anything else.

Doha attractions

Four places anchor most first-time visits: Souq Waqif, the historic market at its liveliest after dark; the Museum of Islamic Art, I.M. Pei's landmark on the Corniche; The Pearl-Qatar, the marina district with a free waterfront promenade; and Katara Cultural Village, part gallery district, part beach, part restaurant strip. Dedicated visitor guides to each are on the way. For now, all four are covered day-by-day in our 5-7 day itinerary.

Signature experiences by traveler type

A desert safari out to Sealine and the Inland Sea (Khor Al Adaid) is the one experience most visitors build a full day around. Traveling with kids points more toward Aspire Park, the interactive 3-2-1 Sports Museum, and Katara's beach; an evening-focused trip leans on Lusail Boulevard and The Pearl after dark. Dedicated guides for each traveler type are on the way. The 5-7 day itinerary already covers the desert day in detail.

Practical tips

Qatar is a conservative Gulf country: modest dress is expected in public (shoulders and knees covered), more so at religious and government sites. The most comfortable months to visit are typically November through March; summer runs hot and humid, which is exactly why so much of Doha's best stuff (the malls, the museums, Doha Quest) is built indoors and air-conditioned. Dedicated guides on weather by month, dress code, packing and safety are on the way.
